Dear Open Plans team,

Your posting asks how we would design New York’s streets if we had a blank slate. That is the kind of question I like to answer by visualizing something, so here is a quick attempt: If Scaffolding Were Trees (mkskitka.github.io/if-scaffolding-were-trees). It takes the city’s permit records for the 3,127 sidewalk sheds standing in Manhattan, 127 miles of them, and redraws each one as a row of street trees. You can let the trees grow for thirty years, or drop down to the sidewalk and look around. It also shows how I collaborate: I prototype early and often, so the whole team is in sync on the creative vision.

I’m a creative technologist with a focus on visual communication, and for eight years my work has been turning someone’s research or idea into something people can experience and interact with, in a way that moves them. That matters here, because this exhibit is advocacy. It has to leave New Yorkers wanting streets that put people ahead of cars, and inspired to do something about it. At The Shed, I turned artist Lizania Cruz’s research into a touch screen where visitors read the evidence and left their own responses. For the Nigerian Pavilion at the 2024 Venice Biennale, I built a generative video system with Volvox Labs for Fatimah Tuggar. This year I built a three-room rehabilitation space with Studio Elsewhere, working with NYU Langone clinicians on every room. Before any of that I made data visualizations for researchers, starting with federal lobbying data at MIT.

Because I build this kind of work myself, I know what a clear brief looks like, how long things really take to make and install, and which problems turn up the week before opening. I organize too: for the past two years I have co-organized Blueberry Swamp, a small independent music and arts festival, and before that I co-produced Wild Birds, a projection and live music series at Brooklyn Art Haus, where I recruited the talent and designed the space. I also know many of the people you would want to hire, from nearly four years at NYU’s ITP as a student, research fellow and instructor, and from the creative tech studios I freelance for.

I would love to hear more about the exhibit and talk through how I would approach it.

Thank you for reading,
Mary Kate (MK) Skitka
